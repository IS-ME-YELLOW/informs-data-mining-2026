## 4. Empirical Evaluation

We evaluate the three main parts of the framework: component forecasting, horizon-specific forecast combination, and spatial residual refinement. All results are county-level out-of-fold validation scores on the training counties, following Section 3.5. Within each comparison, the remaining pipeline is held fixed. The common component reference uses LightGBM with the 163 base features to predict all four components directly, followed by short-horizon target-time aggregation and horizon-specific reconstruction at 24h and 48h. All configurations use the same OSI postprocessing.

Across three fixed county partitions, the complete tree forecast reduced mean RMSE over this reference by 9.17%, 5.65%, 1.51%, and 0.66% at 1h, 6h, 24h, and 48h. Every partition improved at every horizon, with the largest gains at 1h and 6h.

**Initial state and component structure.** A controlled comparison on the seed42 partition first showed that predicting the four components and reconstructing OSI produced lower RMSE at every horizon than direct OSI regression with the same LightGBM features. Further comparisons examined how known information could be built into individual component forecasts. Across three partitions, using the initial-state formulation for the 1h P source reduced final 1h OSI RMSE by 4.52%–7.46% relative to direct P regression with the same squared-error objective. The comparison supports the combined decomposition, weighting, and reconstruction, although it does not separate their individual effects. Reconstructing D from its known historical contribution reduced 1h RMSE by 1.24%–1.45% relative to the component reference, despite applying only near the observation cutoff. Extending the P formulation to the 6h source and adding neighbor features to the 1h and 24h P sources provided further gains. These staged improvements use different controls and are not additive.

Added structure did not help all components. On the seed42 partition, occurrence–magnitude models for the 1h N and R sources improved predictions for zero and small positive values but increased errors above 0.01. Replacing the N source alone reduced final 1h RMSE by only 0.05%, while the other variants did not improve on it. We therefore retained direct regression for N and R.

**Target-time alignment and long-horizon combination.** On the seed42 partition, common-target-time aggregation reduced RMSE by 1.04% at 1h and 1.83% at 6h relative to horizon-specific component reconstruction, but increased 48h RMSE by 0.62%. We therefore used aggregation for the shorter horizons and retained separate long-horizon sources.

At 24h and 48h, unconditional averaging of the six tree sources performed worse than the reference component model in two of the three partitions. Tail gating improved on both alternatives in every partition, reducing RMSE over the reference by 0.56%–2.18% at 24h and 0.39%–0.99% at 48h. The gate retained the reference forecast for about 2% of observations. At 24h, averaging the enhanced component forecast with the gated ensemble improved on the enhanced component forecast in all three partitions, although it surpassed the gated ensemble alone in two. These results support the horizon-specific composition in Section 3.3.

**Spatial residual refinement.** On the seed42 partition, the final spatial model reduced RMSE over the matching complete tree forecast by 2.10%, 0.55%, 2.38%, and 2.12% across the four horizons. MAE also decreased at every horizon. RMSE improved in four of five outer folds at 24h and three of five folds at each remaining horizon. This comparison evaluates the complete spatial module, including geographic inputs, neighborhood summaries, and the graph learner.

The spatial gains were not uniform. All four county-bootstrap 95% intervals for the RMSE difference included zero, and Forest County accounted for 97.3% of the net 1h SSE reduction after county-level gains and losses were offset. The pooled improvements therefore coexist with concentrated gains and deterioration elsewhere. The tree framework was evaluated across three county partitions, whereas spatial refinement was evaluated on one. Both evaluations remain within the same storm event.
