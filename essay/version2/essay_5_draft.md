## 5. Discussion

**Using known information in prediction.** The component results show the value of building observed conditions into the forecast. For P, the last observed outage level scales the prediction. For D, the contribution fixed by observed history is calculated directly, leaving the unknown part to be learned. Added structure was less useful for N and R: the evaluated 1h occurrence–magnitude models improved background predictions but increased errors for larger values, with little net benefit. Component-specific designs should therefore reflect both their definitions and final OSI accuracy. 

**Combining forecasts according to their errors.** Common-target-time aggregation brings together different temporal views of the same outcome, but its value varies by horizon. At 24h and 48h, unconditional averaging sometimes diluted useful high-severity predictions. Retaining the reference in its predicted upper tail handled this tradeoff more effectively. Component errors may likewise reinforce or offset one another during OSI reconstruction. Forecast combinations should therefore be selected by final OSI error rather than any single source metric.

**Spatial gains and their distribution.** The spatial module reduced pooled RMSE, although gains were uneven across counties. Its value should be judged by aggregate accuracy and county-level changes. The comparison evaluates geographic inputs, neighborhood summaries, and the graph learner together, so it does not isolate message passing. A non-graph model with the same inputs would provide that comparison. Utility service relationships and electrical connectivity may define more informative edges.

**Transfer and operational forecasting.** Validation on held-out counties within one event leaves transfer to other storms and regions untested. Broader evaluation should compare event-relative features with the calendar-period code. Operational forecasting changes the available information: a nominal 1h prediction late in this challenge may be days beyond the latest outage observation. Rolling observations would update the initial state for P and known contribution to D, requiring corresponding changes to features and training.
