## 5. Discussion

The current framework combines explicit initial-state modelling, horizon-specific forecast combination, and spatial residual refinement. Further optimization should target the conditions that remain difficult: uncommon outage states, large growth or restoration components, and different outcomes among counties facing similar weather. The experiments and county-level diagnostics motivate four directions for improving predictive accuracy and transferability.

**Richer information on damage and restoration.** Comparisons among counties with similar broad weather and initial conditions revealed differences in outage magnitude and persistence. This motivates testing more detailed infrastructure exposure and condition, vegetation condition, damage reports, and restoration resources available at forecast time. Such inputs could help distinguish persistent damage from conditions permitting rapid recovery, strengthening the information supplied to P, D, N, and R models. Their value should be assessed through controlled additions to the existing features, examining both peak severity and duration. Improvements in persistent-outage cases should also be checked against errors in counties that recover more quickly.

**Learning rare extremes and coordinating components.** The evaluated 1-hour N/R occurrence–magnitude models improved background predictions but increased errors for larger component values. Future work should examine broader training coverage of rare severe states, alternative amplitude representations, and training weights that balance large-error reduction with background accuracy. Joint learning could also combine component supervision with an objective defined on reconstructed OSI. Because component errors can reinforce or offset one another, this would connect the training criterion more directly to the final forecasting task. Candidate changes should be evaluated after target-time aggregation, ensemble composition, and postprocessing, with particular attention to overprediction introduced by stronger emphasis on rare high values.

**More informative spatial relationships and stable corrections.** The current GAT adjusts attention weights across snapshots while retaining a fixed geographic topology. Future work could compare this graph with connections based on utility service relationships, electrical connectivity, or weather-dependent exposure patterns. Comparisons with a non-graph network using the same 205 inputs and with fixed neighborhood aggregation would help assess the additional value of learned message passing. Regularization and correction-strength selection could then be refined using evidence from these comparisons. Evaluation should track both pooled accuracy and the distribution of benefits and losses across counties, aiming to make spatial correction useful across a wider range of difficult conditions.

**Transfer across events and operational forecasting.** A broader labeled dataset covering multiple storms and regions would provide more varied examples of initial states, damage, and recovery. Models should be evaluated on entire held-out storms and geographically contiguous regions, with their specifications fixed before evaluation. These tests would assess whether the component formulations, forecast combinations, and spatial relationships transfer beyond the current event. Event-relative timing representations should also be examined alongside the present calendar-period code. Operational evaluation should incorporate rolling outage observations and weather forecasts available at the actual issue time. In the current task, a nominal 1-hour prediction late in the event can still be several days beyond the latest outage observation. Updating the observations would change the initial condition used by P and the known historical contribution to D, requiring corresponding changes to feature construction and training. Performance should therefore be assessed against both forecast horizon and the age of the latest observation, while accounting for weather forecast uncertainty. Common-target-time aggregation would use only predictions available when the forecast is issued.
